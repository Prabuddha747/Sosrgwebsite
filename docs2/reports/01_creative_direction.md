## 1. Core narrative

A first-time visitor arrives alone, at the exact moment before a single
idea becomes a single word — literature, the seed. From there the camera
never cuts, only carries them forward: the word becomes something made
by hand (craft), the made object becomes an image (art & design), the
image becomes a body moving (dance), the moving body becomes a performer
under a rising light (theatre), the light narrows to a sound (music),
and the sound becomes the score under a lens as every craft that came
before gathers onto one film set (cinema) — the largest, most collective
creative act there is, needing all six others to exist. What connects
the seven into one world rather than seven microsites is that nothing is
ever shown in isolation: each craft is revealed *becoming* the next one,
so the visitor never experiences "Theatre" and "Music" as separate
destinations, only as adjacent moments in one act of creation gathering
momentum. The camera then pulls back far enough to reveal that everything
just witnessed was always particles of the same field, which resolve into
the SOSRG mark — the visitor's invitation to become part of what they
just watched form.

## 2. Emotional arc, mapped to worlds

Per CLAUDE.md Section 3: Curiosity → Wonder → Belonging → Discovery →
Participation → Collaboration → Inspiration → Hope → "I want to become
part of this."

| Position | World | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | *Prologue* (no craft named yet) | Before anything is identified as an industry, there's just one creator and an empty space — curiosity has to precede any content, or the industries that follow read as a menu instead of a discovery. |
| Wonder | **Literature** | Every other craft in this list starts as an idea before it starts as a technique. Opening on a word forming in space is the smallest possible unit of "wonder" — nothing to master yet, just an idea arriving. |
| Belonging | **Craft** | Hands working a material is the most communal, least spectator-oriented image in the whole sequence — a workshop, not a stage. Belonging comes before spectacle here on purpose: you feel part of a craft by doing it alongside others, not by watching it. |
| Discovery | **Art & Design** | Once something's been made by hand, the natural next question is "what could this look like" — form, color, and possibility opening outward. This is the chapter where the visitor's own curiosity about *variation* gets rewarded. |
| Participation | **Dance** | Dance is the one craft on this list that's physically impossible to experience as a spectator only — rhythm pulls the body (or, here, the cursor/scroll) in. It's positioned as the chapter that stops being about watching and starts responding to the visitor directly. |
| Collaboration | **Theatre** | The most literally collaborative art form that exists — writer, director, actor, designer, and crew sharing one room in real time. This is where "no creation happens alone" is said out loud, structurally, not just as a headline. |
| Inspiration | **Music** | Positioned right after Theatre's ensemble reveal, music is the chapter that stops explaining the ecosystem and just makes the visitor feel something directly — the most universal, least explainable craft, right before the synthesis chapter needs that feeling as fuel. |
| Hope | **Cinema** | Cinema needs literally every craft before it — the writer's word, the craftsperson's object, the designer's image, the dancer's movement, the actor's performance, the composer's score — to exist at all. Placing it last among the seven makes it read as synthesis, not just "another industry," and gives Hope something concrete to be hope *about*: everything the visitor just saw, combined. |
| "I want to become part of this" | *Epilogue / Final Reveal* | Not a craft — the moment the accumulated field resolves into the SOSRG mark and invites the visitor in. |

## 3. Visual and tonal identity

The throughline is **handmade materials, rendered with a premium
digital hand** — warm, tactile subjects (velvet, oak, wet clay, ink,
brass) staged and lit the way a very expensive product film stages a
single object in a dark room. Not "warm" or "cold" as a binary; the
*material* is warm, the *camera and light* are cool and exacting. That
tension is the identity: this is craft, treated with the seriousness of
technology.

Three concrete anchors:
- **A Parisian velvet-and-brass theatre foyer at night** (the kind of
  space La Cinémathèque française or an old opera house has) — for
  materiality, history, and warmth. This is where the "handmade" half of
  the tone lives: worn textures, low warm light, nothing sterile.
- **Apple's product-reveal film language** (iPhone/Vision Pro launch
  films) — single subject staged in a void, deliberate slow camera
  moves, shallow depth of field, silence used as punctuation. This is
  where the "premium digital hand" half lives: the discipline of the
  camera, not the coldness of the subject matter.
- **The pacing of a slow, observational craft documentary** (the
  unhurried, reverent close-ups on a craftsperson's hands in something
  like *Jiro Dreams of Sushi*) — for motion tempo specifically: nothing
  in this experience should move at UI speed. Camera moves should feel
  like they're following a process, not animating a page.

## 4. World-to-world transitions

- **Prologue → Literature**: the single point of focus the visitor has
  been watching breathe resolves into handwriting forming in space as
  the camera drifts closer, as if the particle *was* the first word all
  along.
- **Literature → Craft**: the written word's ink texture continues
  directly into the grain of a worked material — the camera pushes
  through the page into a workshop of hands shaping it.
- **Craft → Art & Design**: the surface texture of the finished
  handmade object (clay, wood grain) becomes pigment as the camera
  pulls back to reveal a canvas or studio taking shape around it.
- **Art & Design → Dance**: a brushstroke's arc doesn't end — the camera
  follows its line directly into the sweep of a dancer's limb completing
  the same motion.
- **Dance → Theatre**: the dancer's movement resolves as a spotlight
  rises around them, and the camera pulls back to reveal the full stage
  and ensemble the light was always part of.
- **Theatre → Music**: the stage's light narrows from illuminating the
  whole ensemble down to following one sound, and the camera drifts
  into a space where that single note becomes a full score.
- **Music → Cinema**: the score continues under a lens — the camera
  pulls back through what turns out to be a viewfinder frame, revealing
  a full film set where every previous craft's fragments are visibly
  present at once (a script page, a costume, a painted backdrop, a
  dancer's mark on the floor, a boom mic).
- **Cinema → Epilogue**: the camera keeps pulling back from the set
  until every world's particles gather into the SOSRG mark, holds, and
  dissolves back into ambient motion.

## 5. Interaction philosophy

**Attention, not clicks, is the currency everywhere.** The consistent
rule across all seven worlds: proximity and dwell time — not clicking —
is what causes anything to reveal itself, and it always reveals in the
same order: *sound first, then texture/material response, then
information last.* A visitor who simply lets the cursor rest near
something hears it before they see any detail about it, and sees detail
before any word of copy appears. This directly implements CLAUDE.md
Section 4's sound-and-curiosity rules as one mechanical pattern instead
of two separate ideas, and guarantees nothing ever needs a "click me"
affordance — the reward for curiosity is immediate and ambient, not
gated behind a click.
